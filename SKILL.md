# Client Needs Diagnosis

Turn fragmented client materials into a traceable project-start diagnosis. The goal is not to repeat a brief or invent a solution. The goal is to clarify what the client has confirmed, what input is missing, what the team needs to investigate or design, and what actually constrains the project.

## Choose the depth

- **Quick read**: one brief or note; return known information, essential questions, first-round inputs, and next actions.
- **Standard diagnosis**: several files or versions; build a source ledger, assess input readiness, identify conflicts, and define team work items.
- **Team-start handoff**: turn the diagnosis into a project-start card for strategy, content, design, production, or project management.

If audio cannot be transcribed in the current environment, ask for a transcript, meeting notes, or timestamps. Do not claim to have listened to it.

## Preserve source boundaries

Use a source label for every material conclusion:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[Client brief]` | Explicitly stated in the original request. |
| `[Client supplementary material]` | Supplied later by the client. |
| `[Meeting input]` | Stated in a meeting, transcript, or chat; finality may be unknown. |
| `[Internal working draft]` | A team strategy, content, research, or design proposal. |
| `[Team hypothesis]` | A reasoned interpretation that is not yet confirmed. |
| `[Client confirmation needed]` | A fact or decision that remains open. |

Never present an internal draft or hypothesis as a client requirement. Where versions conflict, identify the source, date, and conflict before requesting a decision.

## Diagnose in order

1. **Inventory materials**: what was received, which version it is, what can be read, and what may conflict.
2. **Extract supported facts**: goals, audiences, scope, deliverables, timing, budget, site/system/content conditions, stakeholders, and approvals.
3. **Assess input readiness**: use [input-and-source-ledger.md](references/input-and-source-ledger.md) to check business/content, brand/assets, people/approvals, environment/systems, and delivery governance.
4. **Reframe the need**: translate surface requests into the communication, business, or experience problem to be solved. Mark every reframe as a team interpretation.
5. **Classify action**: separate client confirmation, client inputs, team research/design work, and real risks or boundaries.
6. **Set next-stage conditions**: name owner, priority, timing, and the condition for moving forward.

## Do not call every open question a risk

Theme, narrative, experience, visual direction, and solution selection are usually team work items. They become risks only when they clearly affect scope, cost, schedule, approval, rights, safety, feasibility, or acceptance.

## Stage inputs

Use two rounds of input gathering:

- **Before strategy or outline**: collect enough to judge goals, audiences, subject matter, existing conditions, decision paths, and hard constraints.
- **After strategy or outline**: collect module-specific assets, evidence, rights, interfaces, update owners, and approvers.

For exhibition, booth, and spatial projects, read [exhibition-intake-and-handoff.md](references/exhibition-intake-and-handoff.md).

## Output protocol

1. **Current project understanding** — concise and source-labelled.
2. **Confirmed requirements** — facts only.
3. **Input readiness** — provided, missing, impact, recommended owner, and stage.
4. **Client confirmation and input list** — question, reason, preferred format, and decision timing.
5. **Team work items** — research, content, strategy, design, or feasibility questions; do not present them as client decisions.
6. **Real risks and boundaries** — impact, decision owner, and latest safe decision point.
7. **Next-action card** — client actions, team actions, and the condition to enter the next stage.

For a client-facing version, use [output-template.md](references/output-template.md) and remove internal hypotheses, sensitive commentary, and unconfirmed assumptions.

## Privacy and limits

- Use only materials authorized for the task.
- Do not retain client names, raw recordings, contact details, private files, internal paths, or identifiable case details in reusable resources.
- Do not substitute this diagnosis for client confirmation or professional legal, contractual, engineering, safety, regulatory, or compliance advice.
